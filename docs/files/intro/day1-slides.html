Course Introduction

Three (ish) pillars of statistics

Data is the foundation of statistics.

  • What does “data” mean?
  • What data do you care about?

What do we do with data?

  1. We collect data (Chapter 1)
    sampling schemes, experiments, observational studies
  2. We describe data (Chapter 2)
    summary statistics, data visualization
  3. We analyze data (Chapters 3-10)
    statistical inference: estimation and uncertainty
  4. We make assumptions about data (implicit in much of this course)
    Discussed in later stats courses, ie statistical modeling

Why is statistics helpful?

The world is full of uncertainty.

(Research) Questions:

  • How long does it take to walk across campus?
  • What will my monthly utility bill be, on average?
  • What percent of people in Minnesota are vaccinated against COVID-19?

We collect data and try to understand what we know (and what we don’t know) about the world.

Understanding variability and uncertainty can help us make better decisions and be better scientists.

Statistics compared to Math

  • Statistics and Math are different fields!
  • The field of statistics involves a process, oftentimes there is not one “right answer” but rather many approaches to answer the same problem.
  • We will learn more about this thought process in this course!

Choices in Data Structure

Sometimes the data collector makes choices about the final data structure

  • Variables can be converted from numeric to categorical
    • E.g. measure household income (numeric) then categorize as below or above the poverty threshold.
  • Categorical variables are sometimes collapsed into fewer levels
    • E.g. no HS degree, HS degree, some college, 2 year degree, …
  • How to measure demographic variables?
    • Sex: M/F or M/F/X or ?
    • Race/ethnicity: how many options? Collapse options? Privacy of results?

RStudio

R and RStudio

R

  • Free statistical software
  • Widespread use among research statisticians, consultants, and others who use lots of stats in their research
  • Allows for reproducible research (can rerun code and observe same results)

RStudio

  • RStudio is an interface with R
  • Offers same functionality as R but in a nicer user environment
  • Plus has some useful additional features

RStudio Layout

Data in RStudio

  • You can open any .csv in Excel or Google sheets
  • We need to load (read) a .csv into Rstudio:
